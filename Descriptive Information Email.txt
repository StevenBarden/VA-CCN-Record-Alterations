WHAT THE RECORD SHOWS

I pulled my own Optum claims record three times over twenty-one months. The three exports do not agree with each other.

A surgical claim of $6,568.00 is present in the first export with its identifier ending 0000, submitted 10/18/2022. It is absent entirely from the second export, a complete 137-claim file. It appears again in the third with the identifier ending 0001, the submitted date moved to 10/01/2024, and a facility name added that was not there before. The procedure contents are identical. The third export is filtered to paid claims. It was paid.

The companion claim of $8,217.00 carries four alveoloplasty line items at $386.00 each — bone smoothing that was never performed. Corrective surgery was done by a different surgeon nearly two years later. Those four line items are present in the first export, absent from the second, and present again in the third.

Two identical denture claims, same service date, same $4,775.00, same procedure codes, same line-item amounts, submitted fifty-two days apart, both appear in the paid-filtered export. Every other duplicate set in this record produced exactly one payment.

THIS IS NOT ONLY DENTAL

The second half of this surfaced the day before I sent it, while cross-checking the same three exports against hospital admissions.

A Tampa General Hospital claim of $19,648.23 shows the identical identifier change — ending 0000 in the first export and 0001 in the second, with date of service, submitted date, amount and provider unchanged. Different provider, different service line, no connection to the dental matter.

The entire September 2021 hospital admission — ten claims across three days, including $40,762.62 to Tampa General Hospital — appears in the first export and nowhere in the second. Twenty-five claims in total are present in the first complete export and absent from the second.

A provider resubmitting an unpaid claim is ordinary billing. A record changing its identity, or disappearing outright, between two exports of the same account is not something a provider's office can do. It happens inside the platform.

TWO MORE THINGS

My portal access was cut off while I was documenting these discrepancies. It has never been restored, and I have not attempted access since, so that no one can characterize it as unauthorized.

Optum subsequently told a United States Senator's office, in writing, that my account was active and not locked. The exports say otherwise.

WHAT THE DOCUMENT COVERS, IN ORDER

Three record exports of one account that do not match, and a claim deleted and rebuilt under a new identifier. A procedure billed and never performed. Duplicate submissions across four providers, one set paid twice. The same alteration signature on a hospital bill with no dental connection, and an entire hospital admission absent from the second export. Portal access cut off during documentation. A written statement to a United States Senator that the account was never locked. A federal confidentiality regulation invoked by a party that does not appear to hold it. The fraud detection system offline for sixteen months, with the alterations falling inside that window. Federal records retention obligations that ran throughout. And finally, a question I raise rather than answer: what happens to the parts of this operation that nobody watches.

That last question is the reason the document ends where it does. Everything documented above became known because a human being was standing in it and able to report it — a patient, a family, a Senator's constituent. Infrastructure has no such witness. A contractor at this scale is required to maintain alternate processing sites, tested disaster recovery, and separation of data between programs that are not permitted to commingle. All of it is expensive, all of it is billed continuously, and none of it generates revenue. It exists only to sit ready for a failure that may never come. If an organization is willing to do what is documented here in the places where people can see it, and absorb the publicity, the question of what is being done where no one is looking is a fair one. I am not answering it. I am identifying it as a place worth someone with authority looking.
